Synthetic Markets, Real Decisions

Evaluating the downstream utility of generative models for trading-strategy validation

Guillermo Enrique Olmos Ranalli

1 / 9

The problem

  • A backtest tests a strategy on one historical path, so tuning many strategies finds lucky ones (backtest overfitting).
  • Synthetic data promises many alternative histories.
  • But generators are judged mainly on realism (sometimes on forecasting), rarely on the decisions they lead to.

What the paths show

Bootstrap replays history · GARCH too volatile (not fitted) · MLP GAN nearly flat · LSTM GAN partly collapsed

5 / 9

Main result

All four positive. GARCH highest (ρ = 0.54); paired tests put it ahead of the others.

6 / 9

Scope and checks

  • 180 of 185 distinct rules are moving-average crossovers; too few momentum or RSI rules for meaningful correlations.
  • One currency series and one held-out period.
  • Closely related rules may make the resampling intervals too narrow.
  • Close agreement with training moments did not guarantee the best ranking.
7 / 9

Limitations

  • The backtester's trade return is wrong (difference of two days, not the holding period).
  • Duplicate rule names: 500 specifications → 185 distinct rules.
  • GARCH not fitted; no genuine walk-forward baseline.
  • One asset, one test period, mostly MA rules.

Corrected code has tests, but the reported results have not been regenerated. Treat the ranking as a pilot.

8 / 9

Conclusion

  • Synthetic backtests carry real signal about out-of-sample ranking.
  • GARCH ranked this mostly moving-average rule set best.
  • Close statistical fit did not guarantee the best ranking.
  • Evidence is preliminary and specific to USD/EUR.

A corrected rerun and broader strategy set are needed before wider conclusions.
